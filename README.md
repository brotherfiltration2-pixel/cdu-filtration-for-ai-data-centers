# CDU Filtration for AI and HPC Data Centers

As AI and high-performance computing (HPC) workloads continue to increase rack density, liquid cooling has become an important part of modern data-center thermal management.

A Coolant Distribution Unit (CDU) helps manage the cooling loop between facility water systems and technology cooling systems. Filtration is an important part of this loop because particles, corrosion products, pipe debris, and other contaminants can affect heat exchangers, pumps, valves, and cold plates.

This guide explains the main filtration positions used in CDU liquid-cooling systems and the role each filter can play.

## What Is CDU Filtration?

CDU filtration is the process of removing unwanted particles and contaminants from the cooling circuits connected to a Coolant Distribution Unit.

Modern AI and HPC data centers can use multiple filtration stages because different parts of the cooling loop have different protection requirements.

The main filtration positions include:

- Primary filtration on the facility water side (FWS)
- Secondary filtration on the technology cooling system (TCS)
- Side-stream filtration for continuous coolant polishing
- Commissioning filtration during initial system startup

## Primary FWS Filtration

The primary filter is installed on the facility water supply side of the CDU.

Its main purpose is to protect the plate heat exchanger and other components from incoming sediment, particulates, and pipe debris.

Typical filtration requirements can be around:

- Up to 200 µm
- SS316L construction
- Washable or reusable filter elements
- Victaulic or flanged connections
- High-pressure industrial service

A properly selected primary filter can help reduce the amount of particulate contamination entering the CDU system.

## Secondary TCS Filtration

The secondary filter is installed in the technology cooling system loop.

This filtration stage is particularly important because the coolant eventually reaches sensitive components such as GPU and CPU cold plates.

Common filtration ranges include:

- 25 µm
- 50 µm
- SS316L filtration components
- Differential-pressure monitoring
- Hot-swappable configurations

The appropriate rating depends on the CDU design, coolant conditions, flow requirements, and equipment protection requirements.

## Side-Stream Filtration

Side-stream filtration provides continuous polishing of a portion of the coolant flow.

Instead of filtering the entire loop through a very fine filter, a smaller percentage of the flow can be continuously treated.

Typical applications can include:

- Removal of fine particles
- Oxidation byproduct removal
- Biological debris control
- Long-term coolant quality management

## Why Filtration Matters in AI Data Centers

AI servers and HPC systems generate significant amounts of heat.

Liquid cooling allows heat to be transferred away from high-density computing hardware efficiently, but the cooling loop must remain clean enough to maintain reliable operation.

Contamination can contribute to:

- Increased pressure drop
- Fouling
- Blockages
- Reduced heat-transfer performance
- Increased maintenance requirements
- Potential damage to sensitive cooling components

This makes filtration an important part of CDU system design.

## CDU Filter Selection

When selecting filtration for a liquid-cooled data center, engineers should consider:

1. Filtration position
2. Required micron rating
3. Coolant chemistry
4. Flow rate
5. Maximum operating pressure
6. Pressure-drop characteristics
7. Filter material compatibility
8. Maintenance requirements
9. Differential-pressure monitoring
10. Required engineering documentation

The filtration specification should be matched to the complete CDU design rather than selecting a filter based only on micron rating.
